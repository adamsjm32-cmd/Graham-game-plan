REP 2

Average vs. total

6 min

This is the big idea on the test. Same speed can mean different thermal energy.

Small cup · 75°C
Fast particles, but only a few.
Big pot · 75°C
Same speed, many more particles.
Temperature = AVERAGE

Add the energies, then divide by how many particles. Both are 75°C.

Thermal energy = TOTAL

Just add them all up. More particles means more total. The big pot wins.

Practice reps: new numbers every time

Each circle is one particle's energy. Use a calculator, just like on the test. Round temperature to 2 decimals if it doesn't come out even.

DUE FRI 10/23 · MRS. MARINI

Trick-or-treat essay coach

You already picked your side: yes, teens should be allowed. Now prove it with the article. Three reps: Wed scout, Thu fill the organizer, Fri write, edit and turn in.

Rep 1 · Wed · 15 min

Level up your opinion sentence

Your sentence is a good start. A strong opinion sentence gives a reason you can prove with the article. Ask yourself:

  • Which part of the article backs up my reason? If none does yet, can I swap in a reason the article supports?
  • Did I write trick-or-treat with hyphens, the way the title does?
Rep 1 · Wed · Scouting report

Where to look in the article

The article has three sections after the opening. Skim each heading, then hunt for these:

“Playful and Carefree”
Find what a family therapist and a 13-year-old say about why teens still enjoy Halloween. What pressures do teens have the rest of the year?
“Not All Teens”
Find the argument about fairness: should all teens lose out because some caused trouble? Also find what an advocate says about people with developmental disabilities.
“The Right Solution?”
Find out how often these age rules are actually enforced. What compromise does the writer suggest?

Pick your best 2 (a 3rd is optional). Highlight the exact words you'll use. Copy them with “quotation marks.”

Rep 2 · Thu · 15 min

Fill Step 2 of your organizer

For each argument, three boxes: reason → evidence (exact words) → this shows that…

The "this shows" box is where you think. Ask: why does this fact mean teens should be allowed? Say it out loud first, then write it.

Bonus move (strong writers do this): name the other side in one sentence, then answer it. Start with “Some people say…” and “However,…”
Rep 3 · Fri · 25 min (two halves)

Write it, then edit it. Two different jobs.

Half 1 · Draft (15 min)
Talk your paragraph out loud to Mom or Dad first. Then type it straight from Step 3 of the organizer. Don't fix spelling yet. Just get it down.
Half 2 · Edit (10 min, after a break)
Read it out loud. Then use the checklist on the assignment one line at a time: capitals, punctuation, spelling, no run-ons, quotes in quotation marks.
